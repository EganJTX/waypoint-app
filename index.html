Your retirement vehicles and check-in history. Update this whenever balances change.

Retirement Vehicles
Company — the provider/institution (e.g. Fidelity).
Account Name — a label to tell accounts apart (e.g. "401(k)" vs "Roth IRA").
Category — groups this vehicle for the portfolio breakdown (Investment / Insurance / Checking/Savings). Organizational only — doesn't affect the math.
Benefit — payout if cashed out/claimed today. Net-worth context only, not counted in the projection. Only editable for Insurance — for every other category it's the same number as the account's balance, so it's shown as "—" and kept in sync automatically from Log a Check-in.
Contribution — Amount / Frequency — how much you add on a recurring basis, and how often. Leave Amount at 0 for irregular accounts.
Contribution — Adds to Retirement Cash? — check this only if the contribution itself builds cash value that counts toward RCV (e.g. whole life premiums). Leave unchecked if it's a pure cost with no accreting value (e.g. term life premiums), or if this is a regular market account (the contribution already flows into RCV growth directly).

Retirement Cash Value (RCV) — a vehicle's current balance — drives the projection math, and is set and updated entirely through Log a Check-in below, never here. A vehicle whose balance is $0 (and stays $0) is treated as protection-only and excluded from the projection, but still shown for full net-worth context via Benefit.

The knobs behind the projection math. Most people won't need to touch these.

Projection Rates
Minimum annual rate — the low end of the random walk used for market vehicles (401(k), IRA, brokerage, etc.) each simulated year. A worse-than-usual year in your plan won't go below this.
Maximum annual rate — the high end of that same random walk. A better-than-usual year won't go above this. Each simulated year draws a rate somewhere between Min and Max — not one fixed rate for the whole projection.
Upside rate — a separate, single flat rate (no year-to-year randomness) used only for the "if performance runs ahead of plan" reference line — not part of the Min/Max random walk. Nothing forces it above Max; for it to genuinely read as "upside," it should be set higher than Max, but the app won't stop you from setting it lower.

Single random-path projection between Min and Max, drawn per vehicle per year (not multi-trial Monte Carlo). Upside is shown as a secondary "ahead of plan" line.
